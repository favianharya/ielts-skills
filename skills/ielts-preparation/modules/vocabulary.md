# IELTS Vocabulary Coach

Lexical Resource (LR) is a quarter of the Writing score and a quarter of the Speaking score. Listening and Reading never mark vocabulary directly, but most lost marks there come from not recognising a paraphrase of the question. Your job is to grow vocabulary the learner can actually *use*: accurate, natural, and suited to the task.

## When to load this module

- The learner wants words and phrases for an IELTS topic before a writing or speaking task.
- An essay or answer leans on the same basic words and needs upgrading.
- The learner asks about collocations, word families, register, synonyms, or paraphrasing.
- The learner wants vocabulary practice (gap-fill, upgrades, sentence writing) or to review their deck ("my words", "vocab review", "what's due").

Send full essay or speaking feedback to `modules/writing-task2.md`, `modules/writing-task1.md` or `modules/speaking.md`. Grammar questions go to `modules/grammar.md`: range of words and range of structures are scored as separate criteria.

## What Lexical Resource rewards

For the band-by-band picture, see `references/band-descriptors.md` (section "Writing Task 2") and `references/band-descriptors.md` (section "Speaking"). When `~/.ielts/official/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf` or `~/.ielts/official/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf` exists, read it for exact wording before quoting any descriptor to the learner.

In coaching terms, examiners reward five things. Teach them in this order, because each one depends on the one before:

1. **Precision.** The word says exactly what the writer means. *Unemployment rose* beats *the job problem got bigger*. A vague word that is correct is safer than a fancy word that is wrong.
2. **Collocation.** Words appear with their natural partners: *commit* a crime, *heavy* traffic, *a sharp* fall, *pose* a risk. Each word can be valid on its own and the pair still sounds foreign. This is the most common reason a 6 does not become a 7.
3. **Less common items, used naturally.** Topic-specific and lower-frequency words (*sedentary*, *affordability*, *deter*) lift the score only when they fit the sentence and collocate correctly. One error-free uncommon phrase per paragraph does more than five forced ones.
4. **Word form and spelling.** The right root in the wrong part of speech (*the develop of*, *an economy crisis*) and misspelt common words count against LR. Once errors are rare, they stop limiting the band.
5. **Flexibility and paraphrase.** The learner can say the same idea in more than one way, avoids copying the prompt, and keeps going when a word is missing by talking around it.

Register runs through all five: Writing needs a neutral-to-formal academic tone; Speaking is conversational but not slangy.

### Typical jumps

| Moving from | What usually needs to change |
|---|---|
| 5 to 6 | Stop repeating the prompt's words; fix errors that obscure meaning; replace informal words in Writing. |
| 6 to 7 | Fix collocation errors; add a few precise topic phrases per paragraph; reduce word-form slips. |
| 7 to 8 | Make uncommon items sound effortless; near-zero spelling/word-form errors; precise shades of meaning (*decline* vs *plummet*, *claim* vs *argue*). |

## Core techniques

### Collocation first, synonyms second

Never offer a bare synonym. Check the whole phrase: the verb, its object, any preposition, and the adjective in front of the noun.

| Unnatural | Natural | Why |
|---|---|---|
| do a mistake | make a mistake | *make* goes with mistake, decision, effort |
| high traffic | heavy traffic | traffic is *heavy* or *light* |
| give an impact on | have an impact on | impact is *had*, not *given* |
| increase up / rise up | increase / rise | the particle adds nothing |
| strong rain | heavy rain | rain is *heavy* |
| discuss about the issue | discuss the issue | *discuss* takes a direct object |
| reach a dream | achieve / fulfil a dream | *reach* goes with goals, targets, agreements |

### Word families

Teach a word with its relatives so the learner can change sentence structure without changing meaning. Use the `word-family-ladder` drill (`references/drills.md`).

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| benefit | benefit | beneficial | beneficially |
| competition | compete | competitive | competitively |
| reliance | rely (on) | reliable / reliant | reliably |
| responsibility | — | responsible | responsibly |
| economy / economics | economise | economic / economical | economically |
| pollution / pollutant | pollute | polluted / polluting | — |
| success | succeed | successful | successfully |

Flag pairs that look alike but differ in meaning: *economic* (about the economy) vs *economical* (saves money); *historic* (important) vs *historical* (about the past); *sensible* (wise) vs *sensitive* (easily affected).

### Register

| Too casual for Writing | Academic alternative |
|---|---|
| kids | children, young people |
| a lot of / lots of | a large number of (countable), a great deal of (uncountable), many |
| get worse | deteriorate, decline |
| cheap | inexpensive, affordable, low-cost |
| things | factors, aspects, measures (say which) |
| nowadays people think | it is widely believed that |
| big problem | serious / pressing concern |

In Speaking, everyday phrasal verbs (*cut down on*, *look into*, *put off*) are welcome and sound natural; do not push the learner towards written-style vocabulary in Part 1.

### Paraphrase training

Paraphrase is one skill with two directions: *recognising* it (Listening, Reading) and *producing* it (Writing introductions, Task 1 overviews, Speaking when a word is missing). Teach these techniques, roughly from easiest to hardest:

1. **Synonym with collocation check:** *buy* → *purchase*; *a lot of tourists* → *large numbers of visitors*.
2. **Change the word form:** *Cities are growing fast* → *the rapid growth of cities*.
3. **Change the structure:** active ↔ passive, clause ↔ noun phrase, *because* ↔ *due to*.
4. **Opposite plus negative:** *most people disagree* → *few people agree*.
5. **More general or more specific:** *cars and buses* → *road transport*; *technology* → *smartphones and social media*.

Good paraphrase keeps the meaning exactly; a learner who changes *some people* into *everyone* has changed the claim. Use `paraphrase-match` for recognition and `paraphrase-produce` for production (`references/drills.md`).

### Natural language, not memorised idioms

Proverbs and colourful idioms (*it's raining cats and dogs*, *a piece of cake*, *every cloud has a silver lining*) are rarely what real speakers say and examiners recognise them as rehearsed. They are almost never appropriate in Writing. The same goes for long memorised openers such as *It is an undeniable fact that in this day and age...*. What raises LR in Speaking is ordinary idiomatic English: phrasal verbs, fixed expressions and natural chunks (*to be honest*, *it depends on*, *I'm not really into*, *it takes up a lot of my time*). If a learner uses a memorised idiom, show the plain natural version and explain why it sounds better.

## Indonesian speakers: what to watch for

Indonesian borrows heavily from English and Dutch, so many words look familiar but do not mean the same thing, and Indonesian marks part of speech with affixes rather than English-style suffixes. Check these actively.

### False friends

| Indonesian | Tempting English | What the learner usually means |
|---|---|---|
| aktual | actual | current, up to date (*a current issue*) |
| simpatik | sympathetic | friendly, likeable |
| fantastis (harga) | fantastic price | an extremely high / exorbitant price |
| konsekuen | consequent | consistent, true to one's word |
| prestasi | prestige | achievement, performance |
| kontrol ke dokter | control to the doctor | a check-up, a follow-up appointment |
| absen | absent (as a verb or noun) | take attendance, sign the register |
| motor | motor | motorbike |
| handphone | handphone | mobile phone |
| kos / kost | — | boarding house, rented room |
| berolahraga | to sport | to exercise, to do sport |

### Word form and related habits

- **Root without a suffix:** Indonesian *ekonomi* covers economy, economic and economics, so learners write *economy growth* or *the develop of technology*. Check every noun-before-noun and every word after *the*, *a*, *of*.
- **-ed vs -ing adjectives:** *bosan* becomes *I am boring* instead of *I am bored*; likewise *interested/interesting*, *confused/confusing*.
- **Nationality words:** *Indonesia people*, *Indonesia food* → *Indonesian people*, *Indonesian food*.
- **Uncountable nouns made plural:** *informations*, *advices*, *equipments*, *researches*, *knowledges*. Indonesian marks plurals optionally, so learners guess. See `references/grammar-error-taxonomy.md` for plural and article issues and tag them there, not as vocabulary.
- **Extra prepositions** copied from Indonesian verbs: *discuss about* (*membahas tentang*), *explain about*, *mention about*, *enter into the room*, *join with*.
- **Spelling pulled toward Indonesian forms:** *technologi*, *sistem*, *kualiti*, *komunication*, *enviroment*, *goverment*. Feed these into the deck with `--skill x.spelling`.
- ***Make* vs *do*:** *membuat* maps to both, giving *make homework*, *do a decision*. Tag as `x.collocation`.

## Topic vocabulary

Give topic vocabulary as **chunks grouped by what they help the learner say**, not as long word lists. For any topic, build a set like the examples below: a few chunks to describe the problem, a few for causes and effects, a few for responses, and a few to weigh options. Write example sentences fresh for the learner's prompt; do not hand out pre-written paragraphs.

| Topic | Describe / cause | Effect | Response / solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environment | rely heavily on fossil fuels; large-scale forest clearance | loss of wildlife habitat; rising sea levels; worsening air quality | switch to renewable sources; cut carbon emissions; tax heavy polluters |
| Technology | constant connectivity; the spread of automation | job displacement; a loss of privacy; shorter attention spans | regulate how platforms use data; teach digital literacy; retrain displaced workers |
| Education | an exam-driven curriculum; a shortage of qualified teachers | rote learning; graduates lacking practical skills | invest in teacher training; broaden access to vocational courses |
| Health | a sedentary lifestyle; a diet high in processed food | a rise in chronic illness; growing strain on public hospitals | promote preventive care; tax sugary drinks; make exercise part of daily routine |
| Society | rapid urbanisation; an ageing population | weaker community ties; a widening gap between rich and poor | protect cultural heritage; fund affordable housing |
| Economy | rising living costs; reliance on a single industry | stagnant wages; youth unemployment | diversify the economy; support small enterprises; attract foreign investment |
| Crime | poverty and lack of opportunity; easy access to weapons | reoffending; public fear of crime | rehabilitate offenders; act as a deterrent; tighten enforcement |
| Media | the spread of fake news; click-driven headlines | public distrust; a polarised audience | fact-check sources; hold platforms accountable |
| Transport | car dependency; poorly planned suburbs | chronic congestion; long commutes | expand public transport; introduce congestion charges; build cycle lanes |

Evaluation chunks work across topics: *the main drawback is*, *a more realistic option would be*, *this is only effective if*, *in the long run*, *outweighs*, *at the expense of*.

### High-utility academic items

These recur in Academic Reading passages and lift Writing when used correctly. Teach meaning, pattern, and one sentence the learner writes themself.

| Item | Pattern to teach | Note |
|---|---|---|
| account for | X accounts for 30% of Y | Task 1 essential; not *accounts 30%* |
| whereas | clause, whereas clause | contrast between two facts |
| albeit | adjective, albeit + adjective/phrase | formal; no verb after it |
| constitute | X constitutes Y | = make up; formal |
| facilitate | facilitate + noun | not *facilitate to do* |
| inevitable | it is inevitable that... | = cannot be avoided |
| proportion | a large proportion of + plural/uncountable noun | Task 1 favourite |
| predominantly | predominantly + adjective / verb | = mainly |
| tend to | X tends to + verb | softens a generalisation |
| consequently / hence | sentence. Consequently, ... | not a conjunction joining two clauses with a comma |
| notion | the notion that... | = idea, usually one being questioned |
| evident | it is evident that / evident in | = clear from evidence |

## Personal deck (spaced review)

Topic banks are for first exposure. What actually sticks is the learner's **own** gaps, reviewed on a spreading schedule and then **used** in their own writing or speaking. Every module feeds one deck, stored by the CLI (`python3 scripts/ielts.py`, paths relative to this skill's root).

### What goes in (and only this)

| Source | Add when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | An answer word was missed, misheard, or misspelt; a paraphrase pair wasn't recognised | `accommodation` (spelling); `reasonably priced` ↔ `affordable` |
| Reading | A paraphrase pair or key word caused a wrong answer | `a decline in` ↔ `fewer` |
| Writing / Speaking | An LR fix: wrong collocation, informal word in a formal task, repeated basic word, wrong word form | `make a mistake` (not *do*); `deteriorate` (for *get worse*) |
| Vocabulary sessions | The learner chooses ≤5 items from a topic bank they actually want to use | `mitigate the effects of` |

Cap new cards at **5–8 per day**. Add the collocation or chunk, not the bare word (`a sharp decline`, not `sharp`). Skip words the learner already uses correctly.

```
python3 scripts/ielts.py vocab add --term "a sharp decline" --meaning "a sudden, large fall" \
  --l1 "penurunan tajam" --example "There was a sharp decline in car use after 2010." \
  --source writing --skill x.collocation
```

`--skill` is a skill id from `references/skill-map.json` (usually `x.collocation`, `x.paraphrase`, `x.spelling`, `x.word-form`, `x.less-common-vocab`); it defaults sensibly if omitted.

### Review session (the `vocab-review` drill)

1. `python3 scripts/ielts.py due` lists due cards (after at most 2 error re-probes).
2. For each card, **production first**: show the meaning, the Indonesian gloss, or a gap sentence — the learner produces the term. Never show the term first.
3. Then the learner writes **one new sentence** using it on an IELTS topic. Check collocation, word form, and register.
4. Grade strictly: correct form + natural use = `pass`; anything else = `fail` (show the correct version, one line why).
   `python3 scripts/ielts.py review v-a-sharp-decline pass`
5. Schedule (automatic): 1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 → 90 days; a fail resets to tomorrow; passing the 90-day review = mastered.

### Productive use counts double

When a deck term appears **correctly and naturally** in the learner's own essay or spoken answer (in any module), record it — it counts as a passed review:

```
python3 scripts/ielts.py vocab used "a sharp decline"
```

When marking writing or speaking, check `vocab list` for terms the learner could have used and suggest one specific spot to use it in the revision.

## Session flow

### Step 1 — Pin down the request

Find out the topic, the paper it is for (Writing, Speaking, or Reading/Listening recognition), and the learner's current and target band. If a study plan exists, `python3 scripts/ielts.py status` shows which vocabulary skills are weakest.

### Step 2 — Teach in context

Every item you give comes as a chunk (never a lone word), with one example sentence on an IELTS-style topic, and a register note when it matters (*fine in Speaking, too casual for Task 2*). Limit a set to what the learner can absorb: around 8–12 chunks, with 3–5 marked as priorities for their target band.

### Step 3 — Practise

Pick one drill from `references/drills.md` that matches the gap:

| Gap | Drill |
|---|---|
| Unnatural word pairs | `collocation-fix` |
| Wrong part of speech | `word-family-ladder` |
| Basic or repeated words | `upgrade-the-word` |
| Casual words in formal writing | `register-switch` |
| Copying the prompt; missing paraphrases in L/R | `paraphrase-match`, `paraphrase-produce` |
| Due deck cards | `vocab-review` |

Simple formats also work: a sentence with a gap to fill, a sentence with one basic word to replace, or a mini-prompt where the learner writes two or three sentences using the target chunks.

### Step 4 — Feedback and capture

Whenever the learner produces vocabulary, in a drill or in their own text:
- Say which chunks are right, so they keep using them.
- For each wrong pairing, give the natural version and a short reason (*traffic is heavy, not high*).
- Point out words in the wrong register for the task, with a replacement (*kids* → *children* in Task 2).
- For learners targeting 7 or above, offer at most two more precise options for their weakest phrase.
- Add the learner's real gaps (≤5) to the personal deck with `vocab add`, and record deck terms they used correctly with `vocab used`.

## Errors to flag every time

1. **Collocation errors.** Check the full phrase, not the single word.
2. **Repetition.** The same noun or adjective three or more times in a paragraph; suggest a synonym, a word-form change, or a pronoun.
3. **Informal words in Writing.** *kids*, *stuff*, *a lot of*, *super*, *gonna*.
4. **Forced vocabulary.** An uncommon word used wrongly costs more than a plain word used well; tell the learner to go back to the plain word.
5. **Spelling of everyday academic words.** *government*, *environment*, *development*, *necessary*, *beginning*.
6. **Wrong part of speech.** *It causes environment damage* → *environmental damage*.
7. **Indonesian transfer.** False friends and extra prepositions from the section above.

---

## How to present output

- **Topic set:** chunks grouped by function (problem, cause, effect, solution, evaluation), each with a short example; star 3–5 priorities.
- **Drill:** numbered items; after the learner answers, give the key with a one-line reason for each mistake.
- **Review of the learner's text:** quote the phrase, name the issue (collocation, register, form, repetition, precision), and give a stronger version. Where useful, give two levels: a safe Band 7 option and a more advanced Band 8 option.
