# Band Descriptors: Coach's Working Guide

What this file is for: estimating Writing and Speaking bands, explaining to the learner why a response sits where it does, and turning a target score into concrete training goals. It restates the public IELTS criteria in our own words, organised around what you can actually point to in a script or transcript. It is not the official text. `references/calibration.md` holds the step-by-step scoring protocol and the anchor scripts; this file is what that protocol scores against.

**Official wording.** If anything here disagrees with IELTS, IELTS wins. Setup downloads the official PDFs; when they exist locally, read them for exact wording before you settle a borderline band:

- Writing, Task 1 and Task 2 (May 2023 revision): `~/.ielts/official/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf` (source: https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf)
- Speaking: `~/.ielts/official/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf` (source: https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)

**Only certificated IELTS examiners give official scores.** Every band this coach produces is an estimate. Say so, and give a range (for example "6 to 6.5") when the sample is short or the evidence is mixed.

**How scores are built (facts).**

- Writing and Speaking are each judged on four criteria of equal weight. Writing: Task Achievement (Task 1) or Task Response (Task 2), Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Speaking: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, Pronunciation.
- A band is awarded only when the response shows all the positive features listed for that level. In the official Writing tables, bold text marks negative features that hold a rating down.
- Speaking is rated on the candidate's average performance across Parts 1, 2 and 3, not on the best or worst moment.
- Writing gives each task its own band from its four criteria. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1 in the Writing paper band.
- The overall band is the mean of the four paper bands (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking) rounded to the nearest half band. A mean ending in .25 rounds up to .5; a mean ending in .75 rounds up to the next whole band. So 7 + 6.5 + 6 + 6 = 25.5, mean 6.375, overall 6.5; and 6.5 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24.5, mean 6.125, overall 6.

**How to apply this file.**

1. Check the gates and caps listed under each paper before judging language quality. A failed gate limits the affected criterion however good the English is.
2. Judge each criterion on its own evidence; a strong criterion must not lift a weak one.
3. Choose the band whose description fits the whole response, then test one band up and one band down (protocol in `references/calibration.md`). Half bands are available when the response sits between two descriptions.
4. Report all criterion bands, not only the total, each with a quoted piece of evidence, and name the one change most likely to move the weakest criterion up a band.

## Writing Task 1

Academic: a report of at least 150 words on a graph, table, chart, map or process diagram. General Training: a letter of at least 150 words responding to a situation with three bullet points. About 20 minutes is the intended time.

**Gates and caps for Task Achievement (check first).**

| Check | What happens when it fails |
|---|---|
| 150 words or more | Under-length is penalised in TA; a very short script also gives LR and GRA too little to show. 20 words or fewer is band 1. Copied wording from the prompt is ignored and does not count toward length. |
| Academic: an overview of the main trends, differences or stages | No overview at all leaves TA at 5. An attempted overview is needed for 6; a clear overview with sensibly grouped data is needed for 7. |
| Academic: figures that support the description | Description with no supporting data, or a mechanical walk through every number, is band 5 behaviour. |
| Academic: report format, no personal opinion, no invented causes | Wrong format (letter style, notes, opinions) limits TA; inaccurate data in key places pulls it down. |
| GT: all three bullets present and developed | A missing bullet points to 4. All present but one thin points to 5. All adequately covered is the minimum for 6. |
| GT: purpose of the letter is clear | Purpose confused or never stated points to 4; unclear at times points to 5; mostly clear fits 6; clear throughout is needed for 7. |
| GT: tone suits the reader and holds steady; opening and closing fit the register | Tone that swings or is wrong for the reader points to 5; small tone slips fit 6; consistent, appropriate tone is needed for 7. |
| Connected prose | Bullet points or note form instead of sentences are penalised. |

**Task Achievement.**

| Band | Academic report | General Training letter |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Every requirement met in full; content slips are almost non-existent. | Same standard: all bullets fully handled, purpose and tone exactly right. |
| 8 | The most important features are well chosen and each is clearly shown with apt data; an occasional small omission at most. | Every bullet clearly handled and properly extended; an occasional small omission at most. |
| 7 | Clear overview; data grouped sensibly; the main trends or contrasts are named; key features covered, though support could be fuller or more precise. Accurate, relevant, right format. | All bullets covered and highlighted, though extension could be fuller; purpose clear; tone consistent and suitable. |
| 6 | Right format and focus; an overview is attempted; key features covered adequately with figures; some detail is irrelevant, inaccurate, missing or excessive. | All bullets covered adequately; purpose generally clear; small tone inconsistencies; some detail off-target or thin. |
| 5 | Addresses the task only in general terms; key features under-covered; detail recounted mechanically or without data; format may slip. | All bullets present but at least one inadequately covered; purpose unclear in places; tone uneven; may dwell on detail and lose the big picture. |
| 4 | Few key features selected, some irrelevant or wrong; format may be inappropriate. | A bullet missing; purpose unexplained or confused; tone and format may be wrong. |

**Coherence and Cohesion** (same scale for Task 1 and Task 2; Task 2 adds paragraph expectations, noted in that section).

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | The reader moves through the text without effort; linking never draws attention to itself; paragraphs are built with skill. |
| 8 | Easy to follow; ideas in a logical order; linking well managed with only an occasional lapse; paragraphs used where they should be. |
| 7 | A clear line of progression from start to finish with only minor lapses. Beyond linking words, the writer uses reference (this, these, such, which) and substitution flexibly, with a few misfires or some over- or under-use. |
| 6 | Overall progression is clear, but linking is mechanical (a connector at the start of every sentence), sometimes misused or missing. Reference is stiff or unclear, so nouns get repeated. |
| 5 | Some organisation, but not fully logical and progression may stall. The reader can follow the ideas, yet sentences do not flow into one another. Too few or too many connectors, some wrong; repetition because reference and substitution are weak. |
| 4 | Ideas are there but not arranged in a coherent order; relationships between them are unclear; basic connectors repeated or wrong; little or faulty referencing. |

**Lexical Resource** (same scale for Task 1 and Task 2; in Task 1 the range is judged against what the task needs, such as language of change, comparison, proportion or process).

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | Precise, natural and sophisticated word choice across the whole response; spelling or word-form slips are extremely rare. |
| 8 | Wide vocabulary used fluently for exact meaning; less common and idiomatic items handled with skill; the odd wrong word choice or collocation; spelling slips barely matter. |
| 7 | Enough range for some flexibility and precision; some less common items; the writer shows a sense of style and collocation, though some choices miss; only a few spelling or word-form errors, none affecting clarity. |
| 6 | Vocabulary is adequate for the task and meaning is clear, but range is narrow or choices are imprecise. A learner who takes risks with ambitious words and makes more errors in doing so also typically lands here. Spelling and word-form errors occur without blocking meaning. |
| 5 | Limited but just enough; simple words are correct but expression barely varies; frequent wrong-register or wrong-meaning choices; repetition and simplification; spelling or word-form errors noticeable and sometimes slow the reader. |
| 4 | Basic and repetitive, or off-task; leans on memorised phrases, formulaic chunks or wording lifted from the prompt; errors may obscure meaning. |

Grammatical Range and Accuracy for both tasks: see the section "Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Writing and Speaking)".

## Writing Task 2

An essay of at least 250 words responding to a point of view, argument or problem; about 40 minutes. Academic and General Training share the same descriptors. Task 2 counts double in the Writing band, so a weak Task 2 hurts more than a weak Task 1.

**Gates and caps for Task Response (check first).**

| Check | What happens when it fails |
|---|---|
| 250 words or more | Under-length is penalised in TR; 20 words or fewer is band 1. Copied prompt wording is discounted and not counted. |
| Answers this question, not a related one | A tangential or loosely related answer points to 4; a misread prompt or no part adequately handled points to 3; barely related content points to 2. |
| Every part of the prompt handled (both views, cause and solution, both questions) | Main parts incompletely handled points to 5; all main parts handled, even unevenly, is needed for 6. |
| A position, where the question asks for one | Reader has to hunt for the position: 4. Position stated but its development unclear: 5. Relevant position but the conclusion is unclear, unjustified or repeats itself: 6. Clear, developed position throughout: needed for 7. |
| Original, connected prose | Bullet points or notes are penalised. A pre-learned essay is not credited; proof that the whole answer was memorised results in band 0. |

**Task Response.**

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | The prompt is explored in depth; a fully developed position answers exactly what was asked; every idea is relevant, fully extended and well supported. |
| 8 | The prompt is handled fully enough; the position is clear and well developed; ideas extended and supported, with an occasional lapse. |
| 7 | All main parts handled; a clear position that is developed; main ideas extended and supported, though support may over-generalise or drift in focus. |
| 6 | Main parts handled, some more fully than others; a relevant position, but the conclusion is unclear, unjustified or repetitive; some main ideas thin or unclear; some support weak or only loosely relevant. |
| 5 | Main parts only partly handled; a position is stated but its development is unclear; few main ideas, under-developed, with irrelevant detail or repetition. |
| 4 | Minimal or tangential answer; position hard to find; main ideas hard to pick out and short on relevance or support; much repetition. |

**Coherence and Cohesion** uses the Task 1 scale above, with these Task 2 paragraph checks: at 7, paragraphing supports the argument and ideas within a paragraph run in a logical order; at 6, paragraph breaks may not be logical or a paragraph's central idea may be blurred; at 5, paragraphing may be inadequate or missing; at 4 there may be no paragraphs or no clear topic within them.

**Lexical Resource** uses the Task 1 scale above, judged across the topic of the essay rather than data description. Topic vocabulary must be precise (collocation, register); memorised "band 9" phrases dropped in without fitting the argument do not raise LR.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy: see the section "Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Writing and Speaking)".

## Speaking

A face-to-face interview of 11 to 14 minutes: Part 1 short questions on familiar topics, Part 2 a long turn of up to 2 minutes from a cue card after 1 minute of preparation, Part 3 a discussion of more abstract questions linked to Part 2. Rated on average performance across all three parts.

**Gates and caps (check first).**

- Part 2: a turn that stops well short of 2 minutes, or wanders away from the card topic, limits FC.
- Answers that sidestep the question asked cost FC (coherence) and, in Part 3, show limited development.
- Rehearsed or memorised answers are not credited toward LR or GRA; score only the spontaneous language. Very low bands describe speech made of memorised utterances.
- Pronunciation cannot be judged from a transcript. Without audio, report P as "not assessed" rather than guessing.

**Fluency and Coherence.**

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | Fluent; repetition and self-correction are very rare; any pause is spent planning content, never searching for a word or form; topics developed fully and coherently. |
| 8 | Repetition and self-correction very rare; most pauses are about content, with an occasional search for language; development coherent and relevant. |
| 7 | Produces long turns readily, with no visible strain. Some mid-sentence hesitation, repetition or restarts show a search for language, but the thread is never lost. Uses spoken discourse markers flexibly (well, actually, the thing is, mind you). |
| 6 | Keeps going and is willing to give long answers, but hesitation, repetition or restarts sometimes break coherence. A range of connectives and markers, not always used appropriately. |
| 5 | Keeps going only by repeating, self-correcting or slowing down; pauses mid-sentence to find fairly basic words or grammar; leans on the same few connectives. Simple talk flows; complex ideas cause breakdowns. |
| 4 | Cannot keep going without noticeable pauses; slow with frequent repetition and self-correction; links simple sentences with the same connectors; coherence breaks down at times. |

**Lexical Resource.**

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | Total flexibility and precision on any topic; idiomatic language used accurately and consistently. |
| 8 | Wide vocabulary used readily on all topics for exact meaning; less common and idiomatic items used with skill, with occasional collocation or word-choice slips; paraphrases effectively whenever needed. |
| 7 | Flexible vocabulary across a variety of topics; some less common and idiomatic items; awareness of style and collocation, though some choices are inappropriate; paraphrases effectively. |
| 6 | Enough vocabulary to talk at length on the topics; some choices inappropriate but meaning stays clear; usually paraphrases successfully. |
| 5 | Can talk about familiar and unfamiliar topics but with little flexibility; paraphrase attempts often fail. |
| 4 | Copes with familiar topics; on unfamiliar ones conveys only basic meaning; frequent word-choice errors; rarely tries to paraphrase. |

**Pronunciation.** Bands 7 and 5 are in-between bands: 7 means every positive feature of 6 plus some (not all) of 8; 5 means every positive feature of 4 plus some (not all) of 6. Score 7 and 5 by checking which features of the neighbouring bands are present.

| Band | What the coach checks |
|---|---|
| 9 | Full command of pronunciation features to carry precise and subtle meaning; connected speech flexible throughout; effortless to understand; accent has no effect on intelligibility. |
| 8 | Wide range of features; appropriate rhythm sustained; stress and intonation used flexibly across long utterances with occasional lapses; easy to understand throughout; accent barely affects intelligibility. |
| 7 | All the band 6 features, plus some of the band 8 ones (for example rhythm sustained over longer stretches, intonation used to signal meaning). |
| 6 | A range of features but uneven control. Chunking mostly right, but rhythm suffers from even syllable timing or speaking too fast; stress and intonation effective in patches, not sustained; some mispronounced words or sounds cause occasional unclear moments; understood throughout without much listener effort. |
| 5 | All the band 4 features, plus some of the band 6 ones. |
| 4 | Limited range of features; some acceptable chunking but frequent breaks in rhythm; weak control of stress and intonation; frequent mispronunciation; the listener has to work and some stretches are not understood. |

Grammatical Range and Accuracy: see the next section.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Writing and Speaking)

GRA weighs two things together: **range** (which structures the learner attempts: subordinate and relative clauses, conditionals, passives, comparatives, varied tenses, modal verbs) and **accuracy** (how many whole sentences come out with no error). Neither is enough alone: correct but simple language is capped, and ambitious but error-ridden language is capped. Writing also judges punctuation.

| Band | Writing | Speaking |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Wide range used with complete flexibility and control; grammar and punctuation appropriate throughout; slips extremely rare. | Structures precise and accurate at all times, except for the kind of slip native speakers make. |
| 8 | Wide range used flexibly and accurately; most sentences error-free; punctuation well handled; occasional non-systematic errors. | Wide range used flexibly; most sentences error-free; occasional inappropriate or non-systematic errors; a few basic errors may remain. |
| 7 | A variety of complex structures used with some flexibility and accuracy; error-free sentences are frequent; grammar and punctuation generally under control; a few errors persist without blocking meaning. | A range of structures used flexibly; error-free sentences are frequent; simple and complex sentences both work despite some errors; a few basic errors persist. |
| 6 | A mix of simple and complex sentences with limited flexibility; complex sentences are less accurate than simple ones; errors occur but seldom block meaning. | A mix of short and complex forms with limited flexibility; complex structures often contain errors, which seldom block meaning. |
| 5 | Narrow, repetitive range; complex sentences attempted but usually faulty; accuracy is best in simple sentences; errors may be frequent and slow the reader; punctuation may be faulty. | Basic sentences fairly accurate; complex structures attempted in a narrow range, nearly always with errors, sometimes forcing the speaker to rephrase. |
| 4 | Very limited range; few subordinate clauses, mostly simple sentences; errors frequent and may block meaning; punctuation often faulty. | Basic sentence forms and some error-free short utterances; subordination rare; turns short, structures repetitive, errors frequent. |

**Scoring rules the coach applies.**

- **6 to 7 is about accuracy inside complex sentences.** Both bands use complex structures; at 7, error-free sentences are frequent. Count them. The usual fix is making existing complex sentences correct, not adding more complexity.
- **7 to 8 is systematic error versus slip.** If the same error type keeps recurring (articles, plural -s, third-person -s, past tense), it is systematic and holds the criterion at 7. Isolated, non-recurring slips are compatible with 8. This is why the error ledger codes in `references/grammar-error-taxonomy.md` matter for scoring as well as coaching.
- **Range gate.** A response with no subordination, relatives, conditionals or passives cannot score above 5 to 6 on GRA however accurate it is. Name the missing structures when you report the band.
- **Weigh whole sentences.** Do not reward complexity paid for with errors, and do not punish one slip in an otherwise clean, varied response.
- Practice and re-testing for GRA errors: `references/grammar-practice-and-reprobe.md`.

## Setting targets from bands

**Read the requirement precisely.** A target such as "7 overall, no paper below 6.5" sets two conditions: the rounded mean must reach 7, and each of the four papers must reach 6.5 on its own. A paper below the minimum fails the requirement whatever the overall. Rounding matters at the margin: 7, 7, 6.5, 6.5 gives a mean of 6.75, which rounds up to 7 and meets the target; 7, 6.5, 6.5, 6.5 gives 6.625, which rounds down to 6.5 and misses it. Show the learner the cheapest valid combinations, then check every paper against the floor. Requirements differ by institution, scholarship and visa; confirm the exact wording the learner was given.

**From target to training goals.**

1. Record the current band per paper and, for Writing and Speaking, per criterion.
2. Find the gaps against both the overall target and the per-paper floor. Close floor gaps first.
3. For each lagging criterion, read the description one band above the current one. The feature that is missing is the training goal.
4. Prefer the criterion where half a band moves the rounded result most. In Writing, a Task 2 criterion counts double. In Speaking and Writing, raising the lowest criterion is usually cheaper than pushing the highest.
5. Express each goal as something observable in the next response, for example "Task 2: position stated in the introduction and restated unchanged in the conclusion" or "Speaking Part 2: a 2-minute turn with no pause longer than 3 seconds".

**What moving from 6 to 7 usually takes.**

| Criterion | At 6 the learner typically... | To reach 7 they need to... |
|---|---|---|
| TA (Task 1) | attempts an overview; lists details; small tone slips (GT) | write a clear overview of the main trends or contrasts, group the data, keep the purpose clear and the tone steady |
| TR (Task 2) | covers the parts unevenly; ends with a vague or repeated conclusion | answer every part, hold one clear position throughout, extend each main idea with a specific reason or example |
| CC | opens sentences with stock connectors; repeats nouns | use reference and substitution, give each paragraph one clear idea, let order carry the logic |
| LR (W and S) | is clear but imprecise or repetitive | choose precise words with the right collocations, use some less common items correctly, reduce spelling and word-form errors |
| GRA (W and S) | writes complex sentences that often contain errors | make complex sentences correct, so error-free sentences become frequent; remove recurring error types |
| FC | loses the thread through hesitation or restarts | sustain long turns where pauses do not break coherence; use spoken discourse markers naturally |
| P | is understood but has uneven rhythm and patchy stress | sustain stress-timed rhythm and meaningful intonation over longer stretches |

**Indonesian-L1 blockers at 6 to 6.5.** These recur in Indonesian learners because Indonesian has no articles, no verb inflection for tense or person, plural by reduplication or quantifier, and an often-omitted copula (details and codes in `references/grammar-error-taxonomy.md`):

- GRA: missing or wrong articles; missing plural -s after quantifiers ("many student"); missing third-person -s; past events told in the present tense; dropped "be" ("the price very high"); doubled connectors from *meskipun ... tetapi* ("although ... but"); repeated subject ("my brother he"); comma-joined sentences; passive forms built wrongly. Each is systematic, so each one alone can hold GRA at 6 or 7.
- LR: word-form confusion (economy, economic, economical); direct translations ("open the lamp", "borrow" for "lend"); informal words in essays ("kids", "a lot of"); the same few connectors (moreover, furthermore) repeated.
- Pronunciation: final consonants and clusters dropped or unreleased, which also hides -s and -ed endings and makes grammar sound wrong; /θ/ and /ð/ said as /t/ and /d/; vowel length and schwa (ship/sheep, weak forms); even syllable timing and flat word stress, which is the band 6 rhythm feature.
- FC: long silent pauses while translating; filler sounds instead of discourse markers; short Part 1 and Part 3 answers that never extend.
- TR and TA: polite hedging that never commits to a position; Academic reports that list every number without an overview.

Practice for each blocker: `references/drills.md`, using the skill and drill ids in `references/skill-map.json`.
